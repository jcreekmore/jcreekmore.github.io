Is there a modern day equal to [Henry
Clay](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Compromiser "The Great Compromiser")?
Every week, I watch several weekly news shows, mostly focusing on politics, in
addition to the numerous political blogs and magazines that I read throughout
the week. Every week, I see the Congress become less and less willing to deal
with difficult problems that are in front of it. Lately, the petty squabbling
is keeping Congress from even performing its most basic duty, such as passing
a budget, as reflected in [a letter from many GOP Senators to Harry Reid in
May of 2011](http://coats.senate.gov/newsroom/press/release/coats-gop-
senators-press-reid-for-action-on-stalled-senate-budget "754 days since a
budget"). In this political climate, is there no opportunity for a Great
Compromiser to rise up like the proverbial phoenix? Over and over again, Henry
Clay helped to broker deals that held the Union together when things were
[looking](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Compromise "Missouri
Compromise") [exceptionally](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullification_Crisis
"Nullification Crisis") [grim](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compromise_of_1850
"Compromise of 1850"). Perhaps that lies at the crux of the problem. In all
three of those crises, an immediate threat to the integrity of the Union was
present. These days, our Congressional Representatives and Senators seem to be
more concerned with simply getting re-elected and pushing the budgetary
problems to the next Congress than coming together on the polarizing problems.
