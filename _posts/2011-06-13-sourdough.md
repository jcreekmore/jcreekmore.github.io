I do not know if it was reading [The Omnivore's Dilemma: A Natural History of
Four
Meals](http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0143038583/ref=as_li_ss_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=jonathcreekm-20&linkCode=as2&camp=217153&creative=399349&creativeASIN=0143038583)![](http://www.assoc-
amazon.com/e/ir?t=&l=as2&o=1&a=0143038583&camp=217153&creative=399349) or [In
Defense of Food: An Eater's
Manifesto](http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0143114964/ref=as_li_ss_tl?ie=UTF8&tag=jonathcreekm-20&linkCode=as2&camp=217153&creative=399701&creativeASIN=0143114964)![](http://www.assoc-
amazon.com/e/ir?t=&l=as2&o=1&a=0143114964&camp=217153&creative=399701), but
recently I got it in my head that I wanted to learn how to bake bread. So, I
did a little research on the web and found a site describing [how to bake
sourdough](http://www222.pair.com/sjohn/blueroom/sour.htm). So far, it is not
going too badly. After a couple of weeks preparing my starter and trying to
get a free day on the weekend to work on it, I finally was able to attempt my
first loaf of sourdough bread on Sunday. It was not too bad. I mean, it was
definitely kind of flat and dense -- I realize that I did not let it rise
nearly long enough -- but it definitely had the flavor of sourdough. Next
week, I think I will focus more on patience and try to give it more time to
rise. Hopefully, getting started on it before 8:30am will help with the
patience bit.
