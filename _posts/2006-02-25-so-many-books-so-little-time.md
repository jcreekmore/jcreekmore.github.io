Ok, so I have finally started to slow down in my book reading. Since I
finished reading
[Freakonomics](http://thecreekmores.org/2006/01/19/freakonomics/ "My thoughts
on Freakonomics") last month, I have not been idly whiling away my time. I
have still been reading fiction, finishing up [Children of the
Mind](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/redirect?tag=manalangcom-20%26link_code=xm2%26camp=2025%26creative=165953%26path=http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html%253fASIN=0812522397%2526tag=manalangcom-20%2526lcode=xm2%2526cID=2025%2526ccmID=165953%2526location=/o/ASIN/0812522397%25253FSubscriptionId=0EMV44A9A5YT1RVDGZ82
"Book 4 of the Ender Quartet") (CotM), [Night
Watch](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/redirect?tag=manalangcom-20%26link_code=xm2%26camp=2025%26creative=165953%26path=http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html%253fASIN=0060013125%2526tag=manalangcom-20%2526lcode=xm2%2526cID=2025%2526ccmID=165953%2526location=/o/ASIN/0060013125%25253FSubscriptionId=0EMV44A9A5YT1RVDGZ82
"Night Watch") (NW), and [To Dream in the City of
Sorrows](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/redirect?tag=manalangcom-20%26link_code=xm2%26camp=2025%26creative=165953%26path=http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html%253fASIN=0345452194%2526tag=manalangcom-20%2526lcode=xm2%2526cID=2025%2526ccmID=165953%2526location=/o/ASIN/0345452194%25253FSubscriptionId=0EMV44A9A5YT1RVDGZ82
"Babylon 5: To Dream in the City of Sorrows") (TDitCoS). Below are my brief
thoughts on these three, relatively short, books. CotM is the final book in
the Ender's Game saga. All I can say is that it is finally over. I really
enjoyed the first two books in the series ([Ender's
Game](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/redirect?tag=manalangcom-20%26link_code=xm2%26camp=2025%26creative=165953%26path=http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html%253fASIN=0812550706%2526tag=manalangcom-20%2526lcode=xm2%2526cID=2025%2526ccmID=165953%2526location=/o/ASIN/0812550706%25253FSubscriptionId=0EMV44A9A5YT1RVDGZ82
"Book 1 of the Ender Quartet") and [Speaker for the
Dead](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/redirect?tag=manalangcom-20%26link_code=xm2%26camp=2025%26creative=165953%26path=http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html%253fASIN=0812550757%2526tag=manalangcom-20%2526lcode=xm2%2526cID=2025%2526ccmID=165953%2526location=/o/ASIN/0812550757%25253FSubscriptionId=0EMV44A9A5YT1RVDGZ82
"Book 2 of the Ender Quartet")) but after that the series went downhill. Orson
Scott Card seemed to get preachy and by the end of
[Xenocide](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/redirect?tag=manalangcom-20%26link_code=xm2%26camp=2025%26creative=165953%26path=http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html%253fASIN=0812509250%2526tag=manalangcom-20%2526lcode=xm2%2526cID=2025%2526ccmID=165953%2526location=/o/ASIN/0812509250%25253FSubscriptionId=0EMV44A9A5YT1RVDGZ82
"Book 3 of the Ender Quartet"), I just stopped caring what happened to the
characters. However, when I start a series, I feel the need to see it through,
so I read CotM anyways. I still did not care what happened, I just wanted the
_Deus Ex Machina_ to hurry up and save the day so that I could move on to
something new. If I had it to do all over again, I would still have read the
book because it did give some really good closure to the series, but it just
did not do anything for me. NW was the first Terry Pratchett book that I have
read. It was quite the refreshing change over CotM. The story was funny and I
really cared what happened. For the first time in a long time, I found myself
staying up as late as I could so that I could read _just one more chapter_.
Sadly, I finished the book in less than a week. Then I remembered that Terry
Pratchett is horribly prolific writer, so I have plenty of his books to choose
from when I want to read another one, which made me happy again :) TDitCoS is
set the in Babylon 5 universe so, if you are not a fan of the TV series, you
probably will not care for the book. I am a big fan, though, so I thought that
the book was quite entertaining. It was well written and seemed to fit in well
with the overall story arc of the series. It was not high, classic literature,
but it was a fun book to read. Definitely recommended if you want to know more
about what happened to Jeffrey Sinclair after he leaves Babylon 5. That is all
that I have managed to make it through in the past month. I have been working
through one of my [nerd books](http://thecreekmores.org/2006/02/11/my-nerd-
books/ "Books of general nerdliness") during my lunchtime at work, but I have
only just started chapter 3 since I am trying to work as many of the exercises
as I can. Next up on the fiction list: [Jonathan Strange & Mr.
Norrell](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/redirect?tag=manalangcom-20%26link_code=xm2%26camp=2025%26creative=165953%26path=http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html%253fASIN=1582346038%2526tag=manalangcom-20%2526lcode=xm2%2526cID=2025%2526ccmID=165953%2526location=/o/ASIN/1582346038%25253FSubscriptionId=0EMV44A9A5YT1RVDGZ82
"Jonathan Strange & Mr. Norrell"). It has gotten several good reviews so I am
excited about reading it. More about this one in a later post...

  *[CotM]: Children of the Mind
  *[NW]: Night Watch
  *[TDitCoS]: To Dream in the City of Sorrows
